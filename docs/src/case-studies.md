# Case studies

Three letters, three traditions, three construction styles — each a
complete, working glyph walked from skeleton to interpolating result,
using nothing the earlier chapters haven't taught.

| study | construction | exercises |
|---|---|---|
| A slab-serifed stem | direct outline, no nodes | line/curve corners, brackets, serifs from expressions |
| A broad-nib o | pen nodes on a closed skeleton | witness nodes, winding, a pen across its axes |
| Malayalam tha | width nodes on an open skeleton | symmetry macros, counters as measures, hand-built round caps |

The studies are deliberately different in what drives them: the slab
stem is *outline-first* with no skeleton at all, the broad-nib `o` is
*tool-first* — the pen decides everything — and the tha is
*space-first*, its advance derived from its counters. Real designs mix
all three registers, often within one glyph.
