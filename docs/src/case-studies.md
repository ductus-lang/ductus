# Case studies

We're exploring three distinct case studies here, each showcasing a unique letter, tradition, and construction style. In each study, we'll build a complete, working glyph, guiding it from its initial skeleton right through to a smoothly interpolating result. And don't worry, we'll only be using the tools and concepts you've already learned in earlier chapters.

| study | construction | exercises |
|---|---|---|
| A slab-serifed stem | direct outline, no nodes | line/curve corners, brackets, serifs from expressions |
| A broad-nib o | pen nodes on a closed skeleton | witness nodes, winding, a pen across its axes |
| Malayalam tha | round-pen nodes on an open skeleton | symmetry macros, counters as measures, hand-built round caps |

We've intentionally made these studies quite distinct in their underlying philosophy. For instance, the slab stem is truly *outline-first*, skipping the skeleton altogether. Then there's the broad-nib `o`, which takes a *tool-first* approach where the pen dictates every detail. Finally, the Malayalam tha is *space-first*, its overall advance stemming directly from its counters. In the real world of type design, you'll often find a wonderful blend of all three of these registers, sometimes even within a single glyph.
